The old lighthouse keeper climbed the spiral stairs every evening to light the great lamp.
Its beam swept across the dark water and guided the fishing boats safely home.
Down in the village, the fishermen mended their nets and told stories about the storm.
A young girl named Mira loved to watch the waves crash against the rocks below the lighthouse.
She asked the keeper how the lamp worked, and he showed her the glass lens and the brass gears.
The keeper explained that a ship without light would be lost in the fog and the rough sea.
On windy nights the whole tower trembled, but the lamp kept turning and the light kept shining.
Mira promised that one day she would become a keeper too and guard the coast for every sailor.
In the morning the fishermen sailed out past the rocks, and the gulls followed their boats.
The sea was gentle at dawn, and the lighthouse stood quiet and white against the pale sky.
When the storm returned, the keeper watched the horizon and waited for the boats to come back.
Each boat reached the harbour safely, and the villagers thanked the keeper for the steady light.
Mira climbed the stairs beside him and helped him polish the lens until it sparkled.
The lamp shone brighter than ever, and the waves rolled calmly beneath its beam.
